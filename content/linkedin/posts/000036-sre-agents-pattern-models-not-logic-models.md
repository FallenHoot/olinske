Every vendor at every conference right now has an "AI SRE agent."

I listened to the CEO of Causely break down why most of them are missing the point entirely.

Here is what stuck with me: LLMs are pattern models, not logic models. They were trained on historical data. They match what they have seen. They do not reason about what they have never seen.

SRE work is full of emergent failures. Novel combinations. Systems behaving in ways that no training set has ever captured.

That is a fundamental mismatch that no amount of context window or retrieval augmentation fixes.

There are things AI genuinely does well in this space today. And there are things vendors are selling as autonomous remediation that are, at best, very expensive Ctrl+F.

Knowing the difference before you sign a contract might be the most important thing your reliability team does this year.

What is your signal for separating real AI reliability tooling from the hype?
