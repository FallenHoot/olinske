I have started to flinch every time I hear "sovereign cloud" in a strategy conversation.

Not because the concern is fake. The concern is real.

The problem is that the phrase usually hides four different questions: where data lives, whose laws apply, who can operate the environment, and how any of that is verified.

Once those get blurred together, companies buy something that sounds safe and then discover they bought slower updates, fewer services, and a side platform engineers try to work around.

That is the argument in my new draft. Stop buying the word. Start buying the controls.

What does your organization actually mean when it says "sovereign cloud"?
